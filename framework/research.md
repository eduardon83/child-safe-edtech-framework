# Research behind the framework

CSEF Level 3 (Best practice) rules come from research on how children learn, attend and stay motivated. This page lists every study and review the framework cites, what it found, its limits, and which rules use it.

> Much of this evidence was gathered for the European Digital Education Hub game-based learning squad (Quality Compass workstream, November 2026). See also `framework/03-design-and-motivation.md`.

## How the evidence is used

- Research supports **Level 3** rules, and adds weight to Level 1 and 2 rules where it agrees with the law.
- Meta-analyses are preferred over single studies. Single studies are used when they test the exact situation (young children, a specific feature).
- Where a rule extends a finding beyond what was tested (for example, stacking several reward layers), the rule says so.
- Popular claims without robust support are named so teams avoid them (for example, colour psychology).

## What the evidence supports

| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Decoration and seductive details harm learning | Supported (meta-analysis) |
| Young and autistic children are more affected by busy screens | Supported |
| Animation in general is bad | Contradicted: animation that shows the content helps |
| Fast pace is the main problem for young children | Partly contradicted: fantastical content mattered more |
| Pleasant colours and friendly faces help | Supported, if they do not compete with the content |
| Red colour lowers performance | Not supported |
| Expected rewards lower intrinsic motivation, especially in children | Supported for single rewards; stacking is inferred |
| Gamification improves learning | Cognitive gains supported; motivational gains not robust |
| Rewards crowd out helping and creativity | Supported in specific experiments |

## Children online and learning design

### EU Kids Online research network (LSE)

*Id `EUKO` · verified 2026-11-11*

- **Topic.** Large European research network on children's online opportunities, risks and safety.
- **Design.** Survey and qualitative research across many countries.
- **Key finding.** Separates risk from harm and centres children's own views.
- **Limits.** Network, not a single study.
- **Rules citing it.** `CORE-GOV-03`, `C-ENG-05`
- **Link.** [https://www.lse.ac.uk/media-and-communications/research/research-projects/eu-kids-online](https://www.lse.ac.uk/media-and-communications/research/research-projects/eu-kids-online)

### Arnab, S. et al. (2015). Mapping learning and game mechanics for serious games analysis. British Journal of Educational Technology 46(2)

*Id `ARNAB2014` · verified 2026-11-11*

- **Topic.** Learning Mechanics and Game Mechanics (LM-GM) model for analysing serious games.
- **Design.** Conceptual framework with case analyses.
- **Key finding.** Game mechanics should map to learning mechanics.
- **Limits.** Analytic tool, not an effect size.
- **Rules citing it.** `A-ECO-04`, `A-PLY-02`
- **Link.** [https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.12113](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.12113)

### Hirsh-Pasek, K. et al. (2015). Putting education in 'educational' apps: lessons from the science of learning. Psychological Science in the Public Interest 16(1)

*Id `R-HIRSHPASEK2015` · verified 2026-11-11*

- **Topic.** Review of what makes an app educational.
- **Design.** Narrative review of learning science.
- **Key finding.** Good learning apps are active, engaged on the learning goal, meaningful and socially interactive.
- **Limits.** Review; criteria, not thresholds.
- **Rules citing it.** `A-ECO-04`, `CORE-DES-03`, `A-MOT-03`
- **Link.** [https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/educational-apps.html](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/educational-apps.html)

## Visual design, motion and sound

### Sundararajan, N. and Adesope, O. (2020). Keep it coherent: a meta-analysis of the seductive details effect. Educational Psychology Review (with Rey, G. D., 2012)

*Id `R-SUNDARARAJAN2020` · verified 2026-11-11*

- **Topic.** Seductive details: interesting but irrelevant material.
- **Design.** Meta-analysis, 58 studies, over 7,500 learners.
- **Key finding.** Seductive details lower learning (g = -0.33).
- **Limits.** Mostly older learners and text-based materials.
- **Rules citing it.** `CORE-DES-01`, `CORE-DES-02`, `CORE-DES-06`
- **Link.** [https://inspire.acu.edu.au/articles/seductive-details](https://inspire.acu.edu.au/articles/seductive-details)

### Berney, S. and Bétrancourt, M. (2016). Does animation enhance learning? A meta-analysis. Computers & Education

*Id `R-BERNEY2016` · verified 2026-11-11*

- **Topic.** Does animation help learning?
- **Design.** Meta-analysis, 140 comparisons, 7,036 learners.
- **Key finding.** Animation that shows the content beats static pictures (g = 0.23).
- **Limits.** Effect depends on what the animation depicts.
- **Rules citing it.** `CORE-DES-02`
- **Link.** [https://tecfa.unige.ch/perso/sandra/pdf/Earli2016_berney_betrancourt_FINAL.pdf](https://tecfa.unige.ch/perso/sandra/pdf/Earli2016_berney_betrancourt_FINAL.pdf)

### Takacs, Z. K., Swart, E. K. and Bus, A. G. (2015). Benefits and pitfalls of multimedia and interactive features in technology-enhanced storybooks. Review of Educational Research

*Id `R-TAKACS2015` · verified 2026-11-11*

- **Topic.** Multimedia and interactive features in digital storybooks.
- **Design.** Meta-analysis, 43 studies, 2,147 young children.
- **Key finding.** Story-carrying animation helps; hotspots, games and extras distract.
- **Limits.** Storybooks specifically.
- **Rules citing it.** `CORE-DES-02`
- **Link.** [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4647204](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4647204)

### Lillard, A. S. and Peterson, J. (2011). The immediate impact of different types of television on young children's executive function. Pediatrics (and Lillard et al. 2015 follow-ups)

*Id `R-LILLARD2011` · verified 2026-11-11*

- **Topic.** Fast-paced and fantastical TV and executive function.
- **Design.** Experiments with 4-year-olds; later follow-ups.
- **Key finding.** Fantastical events, more than pace, reduced executive function right after viewing.
- **Limits.** Short-term effects; television rather than interactive media.
- **Rules citing it.** `CORE-DES-04`
- **Link.** [https://www.frontiersin.org/article/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.583174](https://www.frontiersin.org/article/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.583174)

### Fisher, A. V., Godwin, K. E. and Seltman, H. (2014). Visual environment, attention allocation, and learning in young children. Psychological Science

*Id `R-FISHER2014` · verified 2026-11-11*

- **Topic.** Visual environment and attention in kindergarten.
- **Design.** Lab study, 24 children.
- **Key finding.** Heavy decoration increased off-task time and lowered learning.
- **Limits.** Small sample; a reanalysis found the effect shrinks with novelty.
- **Rules citing it.** `CORE-DES-03`
- **Link.** [https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.01444/pdf](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.01444/pdf)

### Hanley, M. et al. (2017). Classroom displays: attraction or distraction? Evidence from children with and without autism. Developmental Psychology 53(7)

*Id `R-HANLEY2017` · verified 2026-11-11*

- **Topic.** Classroom displays and attention, with and without autism.
- **Design.** Eye-tracking study.
- **Key finding.** Busy displays drew attention, more in autistic children, and lowered learning.
- **Limits.** Classroom walls, applied to screens by analogy.
- **Rules citing it.** `CORE-DES-03`
- **Link.** [https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/output/1370163/classroom-displays-attraction-or-distraction-evidence-of-impact-on-attention-and-learning-from-children-with-and-without-autism](https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/output/1370163/classroom-displays-attraction-or-distraction-evidence-of-impact-on-attention-and-learning-from-children-with-and-without-autism)

### Brom, C., Stárková, T. and D'Mello, S. K. (2018). How effective is emotional design? A meta-analysis on facial anthropomorphisms and pleasant colors. Educational Research Review

*Id `R-BROM2018` · verified 2026-11-11*

- **Topic.** Emotional design: pleasant colours and faces.
- **Design.** Meta-analysis, 33 samples, 2,924 learners.
- **Key finding.** Improved retention (d = 0.39) and comprehension (d = 0.32).
- **Limits.** Mostly young adults.
- **Rules citing it.** `CORE-DES-05`
- **Link.** [https://artemis.ms.mff.cuni.cz/main/papers/emodesign_manuscript_1rev_180928_FINAL_norev.pdf](https://artemis.ms.mff.cuni.cz/main/papers/emodesign_manuscript_1rev_180928_FINAL_norev.pdf)

### Kaminski, J. A. and Sloutsky, V. M. (2013). Extraneous perceptual information interferes with children's acquisition of mathematical knowledge. Journal of Educational Psychology 105(2); and Kaminski, Sloutsky and Heckler (2008), Science 320

*Id `R-KAMINSKI2013` · verified 2026-11-11*

### Ganea, P. A., Canfield, C. F., Simons-Ghafari, K. and Chou, T. (2014). Do cavies talk? Frontiers in Psychology 5, 283

*Id `R-GANEA2014` · verified 2026-11-11*

- **Design.** Two studies, ages 3 to 5.
- **Key finding.** Human-like animals with human-like language reduced fact learning.
- **Limits.** Picture books; short-term.
- **Rules citing it.** `CORE-DES-04`

### Gnambs, T. (2020). Limited evidence for the effect of red color on cognitive performance: a meta-analysis. Psychonomic Bulletin & Review 27

*Id `R-GNAMBS2020` · verified 2026-11-11*

- **Topic.** Red colour and cognitive performance.
- **Design.** Meta-analysis, 38 samples.
- **Key finding.** The 'red lowers performance' effect does not survive publication-bias correction.
- **Limits.** Used to rule out a popular claim.
- **Rules citing it.** `CORE-DES-07`
- **Link.** [https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-020-01772-1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-020-01772-1)

### Dixon, M. J. et al. (2013). The impact of sound in modern multiline video slot machine play. Journal of Gambling Studies

*Id `R-DIXON2013` · verified 2026-11-11*

- **Topic.** Sound in slot machines.
- **Design.** Experiment with adult players.
- **Key finding.** Win sounds made players overestimate how often they won, even on net losses.
- **Limits.** Gambling context; applied to celebration sounds by analogy.
- **Rules citing it.** `C-ENG-04`, `CORE-DES-06`
- **Link.** [https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2013/07/130702100348.htm](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2013/07/130702100348.htm)

## Rewards, motivation and manipulative design

### Deci, E. L., Koestner, R. and Ryan, R. M. (1999). A meta-analytic review of experiments examining the effects of extrinsic rewards on intrinsic motivation. Psychological Bulletin 125(6)

*Id `R-DECI1999` · verified 2026-11-11*

- **Topic.** Extrinsic rewards and intrinsic motivation.
- **Design.** Meta-analysis, 128 experiments.
- **Key finding.** Expected tangible rewards lowered intrinsic motivation (d = -0.36), more in children; informational feedback raised it (+0.33).
- **Limits.** Lab tasks; effects depend on how rewards are given.
- **Rules citing it.** `A-ECO-04`, `C-ENG-04`, `A-MOT-01`, `A-MOT-02`
- **Link.** [https://depts.washington.edu/techdocs/papers/deciExtrinsicRewardsAndIntrinsicMotivation99.pdf](https://depts.washington.edu/techdocs/papers/deciExtrinsicRewardsAndIntrinsicMotivation99.pdf)

### Hanus, M. D. and Fox, J. (2015). Assessing the effects of gamification in the classroom. Computers & Education 80

*Id `R-HANUS2015` · verified 2026-11-11*

- **Topic.** Gamification in a university classroom.
- **Design.** Semester-long field study.
- **Key finding.** Compulsory badges and leaderboards lowered intrinsic motivation, satisfaction and exam scores.
- **Limits.** University students.
- **Rules citing it.** `C-ENG-02`, `A-MOT-01`, `A-MOT-03`
- **Link.** [https://www.smhp.psych.ucla.edu/pdfdocs/gamil.pdf](https://www.smhp.psych.ucla.edu/pdfdocs/gamil.pdf)

### Mekler, E. D. et al. (2017). Towards understanding the effects of individual gamification elements on intrinsic motivation and performance. Computers in Human Behavior

*Id `R-MEKLER2017` · verified 2026-11-11*

- **Topic.** Individual gamification elements.
- **Design.** Online experiment.
- **Key finding.** Points, levels and leaderboards raised quantity of work, not intrinsic motivation.
- **Limits.** Adults; short task.
- **Rules citing it.** `A-MOT-01`
- **Link.** [https://glassmanlab.seas.harvard.edu/annotated_works/mekler17.pdf](https://glassmanlab.seas.harvard.edu/annotated_works/mekler17.pdf)

### Sailer, M. and Homner, L. (2020). The gamification of learning: a meta-analysis. Educational Psychology Review 32

*Id `R-SAILER2020` · verified 2026-11-11*

- **Topic.** Gamification of learning.
- **Design.** Meta-analysis.
- **Key finding.** Cognitive gains robust (g = 0.49); motivational and behavioural gains vanish in rigorous studies; competition plus collaboration beats competition alone.
- **Limits.** Heterogeneous designs.
- **Rules citing it.** `A-ECO-04`, `C-ENG-02`, `A-MOT-01`, `A-MOT-03`
- **Link.** [https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w)

### Radesky, J. et al. (2022). Prevalence and characteristics of manipulative design in mobile applications used by children. JAMA Network Open

*Id `R-RADESKY2022` · verified 2026-11-11*

- **Topic.** Manipulative design in apps used by young children.
- **Design.** Content analysis of apps used by 3 to 5-year-olds.
- **Key finding.** About 4 in 5 apps had manipulative design: lures, time pressure, rewards to keep playing.
- **Limits.** US sample of apps.
- **Rules citing it.** `CORE-COM-03`, `CORE-SAF-03`, `A-PLY-01`, `A-MOT-01`, `CORE-COM-06`, `CORE-COM-08`
- **Link.** [https://www.michiganmedicine.org/health-lab/design-tricks-commonly-used-monetize-young-childrens-app-use](https://www.michiganmedicine.org/health-lab/design-tricks-commonly-used-monetize-young-childrens-app-use)

### Lepper, M. R., Corpus, J. H. and Iyengar, S. S. (2005). Intrinsic and extrinsic motivational orientations in the classroom. Journal of Educational Psychology 97(2)

*Id `R-LEPPER2005` · verified 2026-11-11*

- **Topic.** Intrinsic and extrinsic motivation through school.
- **Design.** 797 students, grades 3 to 8.
- **Key finding.** Intrinsic motivation falls through school and correlates with grades; extrinsic correlates negatively.
- **Limits.** Correlational.
- **Rules citing it.** `A-MOT-04`
- **Link.** [https://www.reed.edu/psychology/motivation/assets/downloads/Lepper_Corpus_Iyengar_2005.pdf](https://www.reed.edu/psychology/motivation/assets/downloads/Lepper_Corpus_Iyengar_2005.pdf)

### Howard, J. L., Bureau, J., Guay, F., Chong, J. X. Y. and Ryan, R. M. (2021). Student motivation and associated outcomes: a meta-analysis from self-determination theory. Perspectives on Psychological Science 16(6)

*Id `R-HOWARD2021` · verified 2026-11-11*

- **Topic.** Student motivation and outcomes (self-determination theory).
- **Design.** Meta-analysis, 344 samples, 223,209 students.
- **Key finding.** Intrinsic motivation linked to success and well-being; external pressure to lower well-being.
- **Limits.** Correlational.
- **Rules citing it.** `A-MOT-04`
- **Link.** [https://acuresearchbank.acu.edu.au/item/8w8z7/student-motivation-and-associated-outcomes-a-meta-analysis-from-self-determination-theory](https://acuresearchbank.acu.edu.au/item/8w8z7/student-motivation-and-associated-outcomes-a-meta-analysis-from-self-determination-theory)

### Cerasoli, C. P., Nicklin, J. M. and Ford, M. T. (2014). Intrinsic motivation and extrinsic incentives jointly predict performance: a 40-year meta-analysis. Psychological Bulletin 140(2)

*Id `R-CERASOLI2014` · verified 2026-11-11*

- **Topic.** Intrinsic motivation and incentives over 40 years.
- **Design.** Meta-analysis, 183 samples.
- **Key finding.** Intrinsic motivation predicts quality of work; incentives predict quantity.
- **Limits.** Mixed populations.
- **Rules citing it.** `C-ENG-05`, `A-MOT-04`
- **Link.** [https://hillkm.com/EDUC_712/Cerasoli_Nicklin_Ford_2014.pdf](https://hillkm.com/EDUC_712/Cerasoli_Nicklin_Ford_2014.pdf)

### Vansteenkiste, M., Simons, J., Lens, W., Soenens, B. and Matos, L. (2005). Examining the motivational impact of intrinsic versus extrinsic goal framing and autonomy-supportive communication. Child Development 76(2)

*Id `R-VANSTEENKISTE2005` · verified 2026-11-11*

- **Topic.** Goal framing and controlling language.
- **Design.** Three field experiments, ages 11 to 12.
- **Key finding.** External goals and controlling language undermined conceptual, not rote, learning.
- **Limits.** Specific tasks and settings.
- **Rules citing it.** `A-MOT-02`
- **Link.** [https://investigacion.pucp.edu.pe/grupos/motem/publicacion/examining-the-motivational-impact-of-intrinsic-vs-extrinsic-goal-framing-and-internally-controlling-vs-autonomy-supportive-communication-style-on-childrens-achievement/](https://investigacion.pucp.edu.pe/grupos/motem/publicacion/examining-the-motivational-impact-of-intrinsic-vs-extrinsic-goal-framing-and-internally-controlling-vs-autonomy-supportive-communication-style-on-childrens-achievement/)

### Gruber, M. J., Gelman, B. D. and Ranganath, C. (2014). States of curiosity modulate hippocampus-dependent learning via the dopaminergic circuit. Neuron

*Id `R-GRUBER2014` · verified 2026-11-11*

- **Topic.** Curiosity and memory.
- **Design.** Brain imaging study.
- **Key finding.** Curiosity improved memory through the dopamine reward circuit and hippocampus.
- **Limits.** Adults; lab task.
- **Rules citing it.** `A-MOT-04`

*Id `R-SHAH2018` · verified 2026-11-11*

- **Topic.** Early childhood curiosity and achievement.
- **Design.** Large US cohort.
- **Key finding.** Curiosity linked to reading and maths achievement, more for children in poverty.
- **Limits.** Correlational.
- **Rules citing it.** `A-MOT-04`
- **Link.** [https://www.nature.com/articles/s41390-018-0099-4](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41390-018-0099-4)

### Warneken, F. and Tomasello, M. (2008). Extrinsic rewards undermine altruistic tendencies in 20-month-olds. Developmental Psychology 44(6)

*Id `R-WARNEKEN2008` · verified 2026-11-11*

- **Topic.** Rewards and helping in toddlers.
- **Design.** Experiment with 20-month-olds.
- **Key finding.** Toddlers rewarded with a toy for helping later helped less.
- **Limits.** Very young children.
- **Rules citing it.** `A-MOT-05`
- **Link.** [https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1458228](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1458228)

### Byron, K. and Khazanchi, S. (2012). Rewards and creative performance: a meta-analytic test. Psychological Bulletin 138; with Amabile, Hennessey and Grossman (1986), JPSP 50(1)

*Id `R-BYRON2012` · verified 2026-11-11*

- **Topic.** Rewards and creativity.
- **Design.** Meta-analysis (with Amabile et al. 1986).
- **Key finding.** Rewards for completion hurt creativity; rewards for creative quality with informative feedback can help.
- **Limits.** Depends on reward contingency.
- **Rules citing it.** `A-MOT-05`
- **Link.** [https://www.frontiersin.org/article/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.00974](https://www.frontiersin.org/article/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.00974)

## Gaps and research wanted

- Direct tests of **reward stacking** in children's learning products (current rules infer it from single-element studies).
- Long-term effects of gamified learning on **intrinsic motivation** in primary school.
- Effects of **AI tutors and companions** on young children's learning, attachment and wellbeing.
- Evidence from **European** classrooms and languages (much of the evidence is US-based).
- Validated **screen complexity** thresholds by age (the four-element guide for under-8s is a heuristic).

Contributions with peer-reviewed evidence, supporting or challenging these rules, are welcome. See CONTRIBUTING.md.
