B-DAT-01
Research use of learning data is separated
L1 MUST		Learning data is only shared for research under a data sharing agreement, with legal basis, ethics approval and pseudonymisation, and never as a by-product of normal operation.
GDPR (Law) PT-MIME (Law)

WCAG 2.2 AA

No flashing content
L1 MUST		Nothing flashes more than three times per second.
WCAG22 (Standard) EAA (Law) EN301549 (Standard) W3C-233 (Standard)
CORE-ACC-04
Moving content and sound can be paused
L1 MUST		Content that moves, scrolls or animates automatically for more than 5 seconds can be paused, stopped or hidden, and audio that plays automatically for more than 3 seconds can be paused or muted.
WCAG22 (Standard) EAA (Law) EN301549 (Standard)
CORE-ACC-05
Colour is never the only cue
L1 MUST		Right and wrong answers, states and instructions are signalled by shape, text, icon or sound as well as colour.
WCAG22 (Standard) EAA (Law)

No manipulative AI exploiting age
L1 MUST		AI features do not use subliminal, manipulative or deceptive techniques, or exploit age-related vulnerability, to change a child's behaviour in a harmful way.
AIA (Law)
CORE-COM-03
No dark patterns
L1 MUST		No fake urgency or scarcity, confirmshaming, pre-ticked options, nagging after a choice, obstruction to leave or cancel, fake notifications, or confusing double negatives.
DSA (Law) UCPD (Law) CPC-JS (Guidance) DFA (Proposal) R-RADESKY2022 (Research)
CORE-COM-04
Purchases are adult-facing only
L1 MUST		Children are never directly exhorted to buy, or to ask adults to buy for them, and any purchase flow is behind an adult gate (guardian or school account), with the price in real currency. This applies to any product likely to appeal to children, even if it is not aimed at them, and an age gate does not remove the duty. Spending real money is off by default, and parental controls work, with defaults that suit the age.
UCPD (Law) CPC-VC (Guidance) CPC-JS (Guidance) KIDS (Proposal) DFA (Proposal)
CORE-COM-07
No disguised advertising
L1 MUST		Paid promotion is never presented as learning content, game content or a character's own opinion. Any sponsorship, product placement or brand partnership is clearly identified as such, in words a child of the target age understands.
UCPD (Law) DSA (Law) KIDS (Proposal)
CORE-DAT-06
Data subject rights are supported by tooling
L1 MUST		Access, rectification, erasure and export of a learner's data can be executed by tooling (admin function or script), and the process is tested.
GDPR (Law)
CORE-DAT-11
Consent for non-essential storage and cookies
L1 MUST		Non-essential cookies and local storage are only set after valid consent. Where consent is the legal basis for a child below the national age of digital consent, the guardian gives it (see AGE-B4-01).
EPRIV (Law) GDPR (Law) EDPB-CONSENT (Guidance) PT-LEI58 (Law)
CORE-DAT-12
Child-friendly privacy information
L1 MUST		Privacy information exists in a version the target age can understand (very short sentences, icons, audio for non-readers), plus a full version for adults.
GDPR (Law) KIDS (Proposal)
CORE-SEC-02
Access control on every store of learner data
L1 MUST		Every table, bucket or collection with learner data enforces access control at the data layer (e.g. row-level security), not only in the client.
GDPR (Law)
A-ECO-02
No pay-to-win
L2 MUST		Nothing bought with money gives a gameplay or learning advantage.
DFA (Proposal) UCPD (Law)
AGE-B3-01
Under 13, access is enabled and controlled by a guardian or the school
L2 MUST		Below 13, access to online games and AI conversational features is enabled and controlled through tools for guardians (or the school in educational deployments).
KIDS (Proposal)
CORE-CHT-06
Voice, video and live streaming off by default
L2 MUST		Voice chat, video chat and live streaming are off by default, enabled only by a guardian or teacher for under-13s, limited to approved contacts, and children cannot host live streams by default.
KIDS (Proposal) DSA-G28 (Guidance)
CORE-INT-02
Schools can take their data and leave
L2 SHOULD		The school, or whoever buys the product, can export all of its data at class and school level in documented, machine-readable open formats: rosters, results, learner-created work and settings, with the schema published. Where the product exchanges data with school systems it uses open standards (OneRoster for rosters and results, LTI 1.3 for launching from a learning platform). The exit process is documented with timelines, and switching costs nothing beyond the subscription.
DATA-ACT (Law) GDPR (Law) 1EDTECH (Standard) EDU-EDIC (Organisation policy)
CORE-SAF-07
Tools for guardians and teachers that respect the child
L2 MUST		Guardians (or teachers in school use) can manage settings, time and contacts. The child is told when such tools are active. Controls cannot be bypassed by the child.
KIDS (Proposal) UNCRC-GC25 (Guidance)
CORE-SAF-08
Proportionate, privacy-preserving age assurance
L2 MUST		Where age matters, age assurance is proportionate, uses zero-knowledge solutions such as the EU age verification app, does not store identity documents and only keeps an age signal. Self-declaration alone is not relied on for high-risk features.
KIDS (Proposal) DSA-G28 (Guidance) AVREC (Guidance)

Proposals and pending law this review relies on

  • DFA Digital Fairness Act (proposal replacing the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive). Proposed 11 Nov 2026 as COM(2026) 1100, procedure 2026/0356(COD), a regulation replacing the UCPD. Would apply 12 months after entry into force. Art. 10 bans manipulative interfaces and Annex I Part III blacklists ten dark patterns; Art. 12 bars profiling-based ads to known minors; Art. 17 gives off switches for autoplay, streaks and relationship-simulating features. Bans pay-to-win and paid variable rewards for minors; paid currency shows its real-money value and is off by default for minors. Checked against secondary summaries of the official text; confirm article numbers on EUR-Lex
  • KIDS Proposal for a Regulation: EU KIDS Act (Keeping Internet Digital Spaces Accountable and Trustworthy), COM(2026) 681. Commission proposal of 17 Sep 2026 (2026/0286 COD). In legislative procedure. Would apply 6 months after entry into force
