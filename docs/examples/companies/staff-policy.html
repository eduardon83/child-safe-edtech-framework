Acme Learning: how we keep children safe in our products

Version 1.0.0 · 2026-11-19 · Owner: CEO · Questions: the Data Protection Officer (privacy@acme.example)

Why we have these rules

This policy covers: All Acme games and apps for primary and lower secondary schools. Children use these products, and children need more protection than adults. The law says so, and so do we. These rules apply to everyone: design, development, content, sales and support. They also apply to suppliers and to AI tools that write code for us.

Our minimum level is Level 2, Forward-compliant. We follow the law and what is coming soon, such as the EU KIDS Act proposal.

We never do this

These are our red lines. No deadline and no client request changes them.

  • We never show advertising to children. We never track children for ads.
  • We never sell random rewards. Children always know what they get.
  • Nobody can pay to win or to skip learning.
  • Children never buy virtual coins. Points and badges have no money value.
  • Learning activities never contain brands, sponsors or products.
  • We never use countdowns, fake scarcity, guilt messages or pre-ticked boxes.
  • Children never lose anything for taking a break.
  • Children never get contact from people outside their class or group.
  • Children never chat in free text. They use preset messages only.
  • We never offer voice chat, video calls or live streaming to children.
  • We never use AI to read children's emotions.
  • Our AI never pretends to be a friend. It does not remember conversations by default.
  • We never train AI on children's data.
  • We never identify children by face, voice or fingerprint. No webcam proctoring.
  • Children's data never goes to a company without a signed agreement.
  • Real children's data never leaves production. We test with made-up data.
  • We never record children's sessions. Analytics never contain personal data.
  • We never collect children's location.
  • Every store of learner data has access rules. No exceptions.

The law and our level also say

  • Stopping points follow the framework rules for our level.
  • Our characters follow the law on manipulative design. No guilt, no pressure to come back.
  • Multiplayer follows the framework rules. Closed groups only.
  • We follow the law on transfers outside Europe.

Only with written approval

Ask before you design these. The Data Protection Officer or the CEO must approve in writing. Write a short decision record: what, why, which safeguards, who approved, until when.

  • Streaks need written approval from the Data Protection Officer or the CEO. They are optional and never punish.
  • Notifications need written approval from the Data Protection Officer or the CEO. They are off by default.
  • Leaderboards need written approval from the Data Protection Officer or the CEO. Class only, with nicknames.
  • Any generative AI for children needs written approval from the Data Protection Officer or the CEO, with tests before release.
  • Research use of learning data needs written approval from the Data Protection Officer or the CEO and a separate legal basis.

We always do this

  • We check privacy risks (a DPIA) before any launch.
  • We explain to children, in simple words, what data we use and why.
  • We delete data when we no longer need it.
  • Every product meets accessibility rules (WCAG 2.2 AA).
  • We never put passwords or keys in code.
  • We report security problems and data breaches at once.
  • We always say when content or a conversation comes from AI.
  • Every chat or sharing feature has report and block.
  • New accounts start with the safest settings.
  • Children can report a problem easily and get help.
  • Parents and teachers have tools to help, and those tools respect the child.
  • We have a safeguarding procedure for risks in communication.
  • We test AI answers for each age group before release.

Our own rules

  • Teachers can switch off every game element for their class.

Tools and words we never use

These kinds of packages are never added to a product:

  • Session recording and heatmaps (Hotjar, FullStory, Clarity, LogRocket, Smartlook)
  • Device fingerprinting

We never write pressure or guilt messages for children. For example:

  • "lose your streak"
  • "don't break your streak"
  • "we miss you"
  • "your friends are waiting"
  • "last chance"
  • "only a few left"
  • and 14 more in acme.yaml

Before you build something new

  • Check it against the lists above.
  • If it needs approval, ask first. Do not start building.
  • If you are not sure, ask. Asking is always right.
  • Run the check on your project before every release.

How to check your project

  • Put acme.yaml in your repository at .csef/packs/acme.yaml.
  • List it in csef.yaml under packs:. Fill in the product's ages and features there.
  • Run python3 tooling/scan.py --repo . --export html or use the GitHub Action csef-check.yml.
  • Open dashboard.html. Red items block a release. Orange items need a person to look.
  • With Claude, use the csef-review skill: "Run a CSEF review of this repository."
  • Add agent-instructions.md to CLAUDE.md or AGENTS.md. AI tools must know our rules.
  • Review csef.yaml at least every 180 days.

Exceptions

Sometimes a rule cannot be met yet. Then you need a waiver. Only the Data Protection Officer or the CEO can give one. A waiver says why, what we do meanwhile, and when it ends. It lasts 90 days at most. Red lines that are the law never get a waiver.

If something goes wrong

Tell the Data Protection Officer (privacy@acme.example) at once. This includes a data leak, a child at risk, or a feature that breaks these rules. Do not wait. Do not fix it alone in silence.

When this policy changes

The policy owner (CEO) reviews it when the law changes and at least once a year. Each change gets a new version number. The latest version is the one that counts.
