# CSEF v0.11.0: bullying and peer safety, and fewer overlaps

Most bullying online is between children who know each other, often from the same class. Closed class groups,
CSEF's strongest protection, keep strangers out but not the bully. v0.11 looks at bullying through two
questions: what the product makes possible (ability), and whether the people around it can respond (capacity).
A product should not offer more than its people can cover. Guide: `framework/06-bullying.md`.

**New rules**

- **CORE-CHT-10** (SHOULD) Reports stay confidential: do not show who reported.
- **CORE-CHT-11** (SHOULD) Children can report for a friend. Good-faith reports never count against a child;
  repeated, manifestly unfounded reports go to an adult after a kind warning, never to automatic penalties; group
  reports against one child are treated as a possible sign of bullying.
- **CORE-CHT-12** (SHOULD) No design that lets children exclude, rank or expose each other: no voting out, no
  ratings of children, no polls about classmates, no children picking teams, no visible paid status or friend
  counts. Teachers can keep two children apart.
- **CORE-CHT-13** (SHOULD) Children can keep an evidence record and reach the national helpline or the EU online
  safety app.
- **CORE-CHT-14** (SHOULD) Communication is open only when someone can respond: hours, languages, response
  targets, and what happens outside them.
- **CORE-CHT-15** (SHOULD, strongly recommended) Bullying goes to the school's anti-bullying procedure, or to a
  default one the product offers (`templates/bullying-procedure.md`). Report categories follow the EU definition.
- **CORE-AI-12** (MUST) Generative features cannot depict real people: no faces or voices into generation, roster
  names refused in prompts, provenance labels, and a priority "this is about me" report.

**Changed rules**

- CORE-SAF-06: after a report, the child gets an acknowledgement, help at once, the outcome, and a "still
  happening" option.
- CORE-CHT-01: channels without text count too (emotes, avatar names, drawings, shared worlds).
- Compass TRN-06 teaches how to help a friend; EDU-02 asks for a short bullying section in the teacher guide.

**Fewer overlaps**

- Merged: A-SOC-01 into CORE-SAF-05, A-ECO-04 into A-MOT-01, CORE-SAF-11 into CORE-CHT-08. Old ids keep working
  in packs, waivers and `csef-ignore` comments.
- Clearer split between CORE-SAF-06 and CORE-CHT-02, CORE-AI-05 and CORE-AI-09, CORE-DAT-11 and AGE-B4-01, and
  CORE-COM-04 and A-ECO-03. CORE-SAF-01 no longer contradicts AGE-B5-01.
- Compass design targets declare the rules they build on and show their status from the scan; they score only
  whether the feature also teaches.
- A `communication` feature group stands for chat, multiplayer, social and ugc.
- The build keeps `legislation.md` and `research.md` up to date. The review guide only says where to look.
- The README keeps only what is new; history is in `CHANGELOG.md`, one short entry per version.

**Manifest.** New optional `moderation` block (`by`, `open_hours`, `out_of_hours`, `languages`,
`response_targets`, `bullying_procedure`), checked when a product declares communication features.

**Sources.** The EU action plan against cyberbullying (COM(2026) 71), EDPB Guidelines 3/2025 on the DSA and the
GDPR, Directive (EU) 2024/1385, two studies on where cyberbullying comes from, and, in the Portugal pack, the
national plan "Escola Sem Bullying. Escola Sem Violência".
