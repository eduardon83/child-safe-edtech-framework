# CSEF v0.10.0: several countries and shared infrastructure

Many EdTech products are built for several countries at once. Member States have also started building shared
education infrastructure: the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium on Education (EDU-EDIC) has been in
force since 4 November 2026. Neither puts new obligations on providers today, but both change what a product's
architecture has to handle. v0.10 helps teams prepare before buyers ask.

**Four new SHOULD rules**

- **CORE-INT-01** Country rules are configuration, not code: consent ages, retention, national approvals,
  helplines, languages and calendars live in a per-country file. Unknown country, strictest value.
- **CORE-INT-02** Schools can take their data and leave: school-level export in open formats, a written exit
  process, no switching fee (Data Act Chapter VI; switching charges are prohibited from 12 January 2027).
- **CORE-INT-03** Sign-in and rosters come from the school: the school's identity provider or an LTI 1.3
  launch, the minimum roster fields, pseudonymous learner IDs.
- **CORE-INT-04** Critical dependencies are declared and replaceable: where each provider is established, a
  fallback for critical providers outside the EU or EEA, AI models behind one internal interface.

**Checks.** Three manifest checks (`country_config_missing`, `third_party_jurisdiction_missing`,
`critical_dependency_without_fallback`) and a pattern for consent ages written into code. New manifest fields:
`country_config`, and `jurisdiction`, `critical` and `fallback` on third parties.

**Guide.** `framework/05-several-countries.md` explains EDU-EDIC, the Data Act and national differences, with a
six-step checklist for product teams.
