For designers and educators

Show what your product teaches

The Literacy Compass reads your design documents, teacher guides and in-product text. It shows what the product teaches about digital, media and AI literacy, and how you know.

  • 45 targets on 8 axes, from DigComp 3.0, AILit, UNESCO and EU policy.
  • Flags claims with nothing behind them, and copy that teaches the opposite.
  • Shows the next three steps.
  • Maps your evidence to the EU guidelines on digital education content, Swedish Edtech's Kvalitetsportfolio and UNICEF's EdTech for Good.

A profile, never a score. No total, no ranking, no badge.

See an example:HTMLCompass reportDesign for learning guide

Evidence ladder, for each target

0Not found. Nothing addresses it.
1Stated. A goal is written down.
2Designed. Activities are built for it.
3Tested. Tried with learners.
4Measured. Outcomes are measured.

Plans do not count.

For teams preparing a label

Describe your product. Submit it to any label.

CSEF already checks what your product does with children's data, money and attention. Turn that work into a label package: standard metadata, scan results, a serious-game pre-check and your evidence, in one file.

1 Describe

Add a short metadata block to csef.yaml.

2 Check

Run the scan, the Compass and the pre-check.

3 Package

One command builds the zip, with a hash for every file.

4 Submit

Choose a label. The label body assesses and replies.

22 criteria, checked before you apply

For serious games, the pre-check uses the criteria of Caserman et al. (2020): the serious part, the game part and the balance between them. CSEF checks many of them already. For the rest, your team writes a short statement and points to evidence.

5checked by CSEF10in part5need a statement2limited for children

The package describes the product, never the children who use it. No learner data.

Formats: SG-MDF (DIN SPEC 91380) as JSON and XML for serious-game catalogues, and schema.org LearningResource for any learning product.

For researchers and data teams

Collect learning data. Publish only what is safe.

Learning games can show where pupils get stuck and whether they learn. CSEF adopts the Open Game Data event standard and adds the limits EU children need: what to log, how to make player codes, when research may run, and what may go public.

A child-safe event profileField by field: random codes for one product, declared events only, no free text, one device class.

Checks for event files and feedsFind undeclared events, free text, emails and hashed ids. Check a feed before you publish it.

An open evidence feedTime per level, where pupils get stuck, help, learning gain, accessibility, natural stops. At least 10 players and 3 classes behind every figure.

Questions

Is CSEF a standard or a certification?

No. It is a framework, published as a draft for public comment. It is not legal advice and not a certification.

Do I need to be a developer?

No. The Workbench runs the checks that do not need code in your browser: add your product description and documents, and download the reports. Companies set their boundaries with the questionnaire.

What does it cost?

Nothing. Framework content is CC BY 4.0 and code is Apache 2.0. Label bodies set their own fees, if any.

Is the pre-check a label?

No. It shows what is ready and what is missing. Only a label body can award its label, after its own assessment.

What data leaves my company?

The tools run on your own machines, and the Workbench and questionnaire run inside your browser. When submissions open, only a label package you choose to send will leave, and it holds no learner data.

How can I give feedback?

Open an issue on GitHub. CSEF is a draft, and comments shape the next version.
